Layered Architecture · Metrology Core

High-Fidelity Horological Engineering

Engineered from first principles with strict separation of concerns: lock-free audio capture, pure NumPy mathematical core, and total algorithmic transparency without black boxes.

Layer 1 & 2 · Audio & DSP

Pure NumPy Acoustic DSP Core

Digital signal processing 100% written in Python/NumPy without runtime dependencies on SciPy or C++ extensions. Lock-free SPSC (Single-Producer Single-Consumer) ring buffer with absolute sample indexing synchronized to the ADC hardware clock.

// SPSC Ring Buffer: Zero Audio Loss
index_abs = total_samples + write_head
residuals = t_host - (n / f_s)
  • Dedicated child process for audio capture (native PortAudio).
  • GUI operations never delay or block audio sample ingestion.
  • Automated buffer overwrite detection (`SamplesOverwritten`).
Layer 2 · Beat Detector

Swiss Lever Tri-Pulse Analysis

Precise acoustic identification of the three discrete physical events produced during each alternation by the balance wheel, pallet fork, and escape wheel. Sub-sample temporal resolution with automatic noise floor rejection.

t_lift = P3 - P1 (Lift Duration)
A = LA / (2 · sin(π · f · t_lift))
  • P1 (Unlocking): Impulse pin enters the fork notch.
  • P2 (Impulse): Escape tooth slides over pallet impulse face.
  • P3 (Drop & Lock): Tooth hits locking face and fork hits banking pin.
Layer 3 · Metrics Engine

Weighted Parity Regression

Rate and beat error calculation using robust linear regression weighted by tic/tac parity with 5σ trimming (MAD outlier filter), eliminating false deviations from external ambient shocks.

t_k = a + P·k + c·(-1)^k
Rate = (P_nom / P - 1) · 86400 [s/d]
Beat Error = 2·|c| [strictly evaluated at P1]
  • Beat error measured at P1 for pure balance symmetry isolation.
  • Independent tracking of mean amplitude, tic amplitude, and tac amplitude.
  • Sliding measurement windows from 10 s to 60 s with 4 Hz telemetry refresh.
Layer 4 · Hardware Clock

ADC Clock Drift Compensation

Consumer USB sound card crystal oscillators carry tolerances of ±30 to ±100 ppm (where 1 ppm = 0.0864 s/d). Advanced Timegrapher measures and eliminates this systematic hardware bias.

Swiss Lever Escapement Physics

The Three Acoustic Impulses (P1, P2, P3)

Amplitude: A = LA / [2 · sin(π · f · t_lift)]

In every half-oscillation, the escapement produces three sequential acoustic transients. Measuring the sub-millisecond interval between P1 and P3 is the physical foundation required to calculate the balance wheel amplitude without invasive optical teardowns.

P1 · Unlocking
Impulse Pin Fork Entry

The balance wheel impulse pin strikes the fork notch and moves the pallet to release the locked escape tooth. This is the only sound directly produced by balance wheel inertia. For this reason, true horological Beat Error is measured strictly at P1.

P2 · Impulse
Energy Transfer Phase

The escape tooth slides along the impulse face of the pallet jewel, transferring energy to maintain oscillation. It is an irregular acoustic glide that laboratory Witschi meters omit when computing the lift angle window.

P3 · Drop & Lock
Impact on Pallet & Banking Pin

The escape wheel tooth drops onto the opposing pallet stone and the pallet fork hits the banking pin. This is acoustically the most energetic impact shock wave, serving as the timing anchor in detection.
